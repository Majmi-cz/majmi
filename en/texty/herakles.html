HERACLES

dialogue 2026 written: in my head
Heracles?
more like HER ANKLES

characters

Heracles – a strong, famous demigod, needs no introduction.

Slave – a lonely, worn-out woman who mocks Heracles even though she is a slave.


setting

An ancient Greek market. Hundreds of people, hundreds of stories. Everyone wants to shop and nobody cares about anyone else, however strong, or however much a mere slave. The paradox is that the two of them get a dialogue, though one is this and the other is that.

(The Athenian market, full of people and above all the loud cries of merchants offering fruit or armour at half what the next stall asks. At one moment Heracles bursts into the middle of it. He flexes every muscle like a marble statue, but the crowd doesn’t lock eyes.)

HeraclesWho are you gawking at, hag?!

SlaveIsn’t that what you wanted? The attention and admiration of the crowd?

HeraclesWhat do you know about it? And why do you count yourself as the crowd, anyway?

SlaveA crowd pays attention to you, which I’m doing. But if I bother you that much –

HeraclesGo ahead and look, crowd!

SlaveAll of a sudden… Isn’t that ironic?

HeraclesWhat do you mean?

SlaveI’m a slave, but you’re a slave too, by the look of it.

HeraclesLuckily it only “looks that way”!

Slave“Only looks.”

HeraclesWhy are you taking my words literally again?

SlaveYou just said it only looks that way. I’d call that a metaphor.

HeraclesA metaphor? As in, figuratively, that I only look strong?

SlaveYou’re not stupid.

HeraclesThank you…

(The slave woman starts cackling.)

SlaveYou call that strength? Thanking an old rag at the market?

HeraclesIsn’t that the lesson you want to teach me?

SlaveA lesson? What are you talking about? You’re looking for a lesson behind the mockery?

HeraclesWell, yes… That’s what you wanted.

SlaveI wanted to provoke you! Haha, just look, you’re a laughing stock!

(The slave woman is right. Suddenly everyone is watching. Heracles doesn’t understand why. When he was showing off in top form, nobody cared, but now…)

SlaveWell then, you lout, now you’ve got a crowd admiring you!

HeraclesThat’s not funny.

SlaveDid it seem funny at Omphale’s? In a skirt, with a distaff in your hand?

(The crowds start whispering. Why is the muscleman talking to the rabble? Why is the rabble bothering with a demigod? Why does Heracles care?)

SlaveIn the end people only care when you fall, Icarus.

HeraclesI’m – not –

SlaveWith that many muscles and no feeling, you’ll fall even sooner.

HeraclesYou can’t – mean that.

(Heracles is on the verge of tears. He might actually cry, even though he is an undefeated champion, a mountain of muscle and meat, the manliest soldier there is.)

SlaveWhat… are you going to cry?

HeraclesThat is the strength – to cry here and now.

(Heracles really did burst into tears in front of everyone. The crowds whispered louder and louder. Nobody cared who was crying. Everyone cared that he was crying. It’s unbelievable that a rag like that could make someone cry, especially a demigod, or a man.)
