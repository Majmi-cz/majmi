VELVET AND SMETANA

essay 2026 written: in my head

Why do foreigners tell me I’m a good person? That WE ONCE had a revolution with no lives lost! I wasn’t there – what are they praising me for? I heard about it on TV, same as them. Maybe the teachers mentioned it at school, or even back in kindergarten.

When they asked me what I think about it – it’s true that I like that demonstration, I like the students’ speeches, but I still said what I’m writing now. I’m quoting myself. The others answered by asking whether I’m really not a patriot. Yes, I love Czechia, I love the republic and its history – but I’m not some neo-Nazi who makes it his whole personality. Czechs “aren’t the best”, not at all.

How can I judge others, other people, or even myself, based on some someone scribbling on a map with a thick marker? And some of them do it with a ruler, they’re the worst. You have a beautiful lake, a river, and you split it with the ruler of two tyrants. Mostly the mountains got traced with that marker, but my point stays the same – I wasn’t even born when people were demonstrating.

What does it have to do with me, apart from the fact that I was born a few kilometres from there and not somewhere in Africa or the middle of nowhere? And I’m supposed to be proud of that? Of my parents being local? How much credit do I deserve for my own luck? Not much, I think – it’s out of my control.

And it really bothers me that there are monsters walking among us who claim things or judge others based on that marker on a map or a colour on a flag. I don’t mind symbols dividing people; the problem is that they sort them into boxes by chance. Just like when the teacher picks your classmates for a project worth 50 % of your grade. In football, too, you support the team you’ve supported since childhood, or because it’s your village’s team.

And then we can go person by person. Take Smetana. I don’t like classical music and I don’t plan to listen to classical music – but I’m supposed to be proud he’s the same nationality? He was literally just famous, and that’s why we learn about him, not because I enjoy classical music. The hole in that logic is gigantic.

Can a nation actually own the mountains, anyway? I think the hill couldn’t care less – and the animals don’t give a damn either.

“I should love Mácha because he was Czech” – also bullshit. I love him because he wrote in Czech like me, but, unlike me, damn well. And that kind of relationship between authors is better than “a Czech is a Czech” (assuming we’ll be taught about them). But stars like that should be mentioned not because they were Czech, but because they wrote in Czech.

A Czech isn’t someone who was born here, or someone who drinks the most beer. A Czech is someone who calls himself one.
